The Pythagorean theorem is one of the most famous results in geometry. It describes the relationship between the sides of a right triangle. The longest side of a right triangle is called the hypotenuse, and it is always opposite the right angle. The theorem states that the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides. It is usually written as a^2 + b^2 = c^2, where c is the hypotenuse. For example, a triangle with sides 3 and 4 has a hypotenuse of length 5, because 9 + 16 = 25. The theorem is named after the Greek mathematician Pythagoras, although it was known earlier in Babylon and India. It is used to compute distances, for example the distance between two points on a plane.
