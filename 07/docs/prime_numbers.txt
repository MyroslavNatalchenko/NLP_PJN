A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has exactly two divisors: 1 and itself. The first prime numbers are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 and 13. The number 2 is the only even prime number. A natural number greater than 1 that is not prime is called a composite number, for example 4, 6 or 9. The fundamental theorem of arithmetic says that every natural number greater than 1 can be written as a product of prime numbers in exactly one way, for example 12 = 2 * 2 * 3. Around 300 BC the Greek mathematician Euclid proved that there are infinitely many prime numbers. A simple method for finding all primes up to a given number is the Sieve of Eratosthenes. Prime numbers are very important in cryptography, for example in the RSA algorithm.
